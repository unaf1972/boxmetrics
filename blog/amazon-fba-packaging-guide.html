Amazon FBA Packaging Guide

Ship smarter to FBA. Learn the rules, avoid the fees, and protect your margins.

Shipping to Amazon FBA is where most new sellers lose money without realizing it. The wrong box size, an overlooked prep requirement, or a miscalculated DIM weight can quietly turn a profitable product into a loss-making one.

This guide covers everything you need to know about FBA packaging in 2026 — the rules, the math, and the practical tips that keep your margins healthy.

Why FBA Packaging Matters More Than You Think

Amazon charges you on two fronts: the shipping you pay to get inventory to a fulfillment center, and the storage fees once it's there. Both are directly affected by how you package your products.

A box that's 2 inches too big in each dimension can increase shipping costs by 30–50% through dimensional weight alone. Over a year of shipments, that adds up to thousands of dollars.

FBA Box Size and Weight Rules

Amazon has specific limits for boxes sent to fulfillment centers:

Maximum box weight (standard)50 lbs

Maximum shipment weight (LTL)1,500 lbs per pallet

Exceed these and Amazon charges you an unplanned service fee — a penalty that ranges from $10 to $100+ depending on the violation. Getting the box size right the first time is far cheaper than fixing it after the fact.

How Dimensional Weight Destroys FBA Margins

This is the single most overlooked cost in FBA shipping. Amazon (and the carriers they use) apply dimensional weight to every box. If your package takes up more space than its weight justifies, you pay for the space, not the weight.

The formula is the same one used by UPS, FedEx, and DHL:

DIM Weight = (Length × Width × Height) ÷ 139

Here's a real example. You're shipping a box that measures 20" × 16" × 12" and actually weighs 8 lbs:

  • Volume: 20 × 16 × 12 = 3,840 cubic inches
  • DIM Weight: 3,840 ÷ 139 = 27.6 lbs
  • Billable weight: 28 lbs (rounded up)

You shipped 8 lbs of product, but you're paying for 28 lbs of shipping. That's a 250% increase — and it's entirely due to box size.

Amazon FBA Prep Requirements

Every item you send to FBA must meet Amazon's prep requirements. Missing prep is one of the most common causes of shipment rejections and delays.

Poly bagging

Required for:

  • Items that could be damaged by dust, moisture, or handling
  • Loose items (jewelry, small parts, fabric)
  • Any item that could be mistaken for a set or kit
  • Liquids in containers under 4 oz

Poly bags must have a suffocation warning label if the opening is 5 inches or larger.

Bubble wrap

Required for fragile items, glass, ceramics, and electronics. Use at least 2 inches of padding on all sides.

FNSKU labeling

Every unit needs an FNSKU barcode. Amazon offers this as a service (for a fee) but doing it yourself costs pennies.

Sets and bundles

If you're selling multiple items as a set, they must be shrink-wrapped or bagged together and labeled as a single unit.

Reducing FBA Shipping Costs: 7 Practical Tips

  1. Right-size every box. Test different box dimensions before committing to a shipment. A 1-inch reduction on each side can mean thousands in annual savings.
  2. Use our Box Fill Calculator. It tells you exactly how many products fit in a given box and how much void fill you're paying to ship.
  3. Ship full pallets when possible. Pallet shipments always cost less per unit than individual boxes. Use our Pallet Layer Calculator to maximize cartons per pallet.
  4. Consolidate shipments. If you have multiple SKUs going to the same fulfillment center, ship them together instead of separately.
  5. Consider FBA's partnered carrier program. Amazon negotiates lower rates with UPS, FedEx, and other carriers. Use it whenever eligible.
  6. Monitor IPI score. Your Inventory Performance Index affects storage limits and fees. Poor packaging choices can hurt it.
  7. Avoid peak-season surcharges. Ship in September or October rather than November if you can — Q4 surcharges can add 20–30% to shipping costs.

FBA Packaging Materials Checklist

Keep these on hand for smooth shipments:

  • Corrugated boxes in multiple sizes
  • Poly mailers (multiple sizes)
  • Bubble wrap
  • Air pillows
  • Kraft paper or void fill
  • Packing tape (water-activated for security)
  • FNSKU thermal labels
  • Scale (for accurate weight readings)

The Bottom Line

FBA is a powerful business model, but it rewards sellers who understand the math. Dimensional weight, box size limits, and prep requirements aren't just rules — they're the difference between 20% margins and 2% margins.
